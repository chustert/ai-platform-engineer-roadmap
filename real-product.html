Do the work on a real product

Most learning roadmaps have you build demo projects that nobody uses. Hiring managers for both kinds of role see through that. Product postings ask whether you have "shipped a product that other people use". Staff postings ask whether you have "actually operated non-deterministic LLM workloads in production". A real product answers both questions.

Pick one AI product with real users and do the work of Phases 2 to 10 on it: model cost, retrieval, tracing, evaluations, reliability, and security. Traces of real requests, the real cost of each task, retrieval failures on real content, and a regression suite built from real complaints all prove more than the same artifacts built on sample data. The improvements also help the product.

Find a product to work on

This list goes from the strongest evidence to the weakest:

  1. A product you already own or run.
  2. Your employer's product, if you have permission to work on its AI features and to talk about the results.
  3. An open-source project with active users.
  4. A small product you ship yourself in the first two months. Good options are AI search and answers for a community or business website, an assistant for a local organisation, or a tool you would use yourself.

A product that answers questions over a set of documents is a good choice, because it touches almost every phase. The rest of the roadmap calls it "your product".

Split the work between your product and Forge

Forge is the capstone, an internal platform for engineers who build with agents. The table shows which work goes where.

PhaseOn your productOn Forge
2. Model costCost and latency for each task, model routing, and prompt caching.One model gateway for all agents.
3. RetrievalRetrieval evals from real queries, freshness, and re-indexing.A context engine over repositories and docs.
4. Tool serversAn MCP server that gives agents access to the product's main feature.An MCP server with engineering tools.
7. TracingTraces and cost dashboards in production.A dashboard for agent operations.
8. EvaluationsAn answer-quality benchmark that every prompt or model change must pass.A benchmark for pull request (PR) reviews.
9 and 10. Reliability and securityA plan for provider outages, and prompt-injection tests that use content the product ingests.Budgets, sandboxes, and approvals.

Use Forge yourself every day, on your product and your other work, so that Forge also has a real user. If a team you work with agrees, try parts of Forge with them, such as a shared agent setup, a PR-review agent, or an MCP server. That is the closest practice you can get for the Staff role's requirement to drive adoption through pairing instead of mandates.

Talk to your users

Talk to your product's users throughout the year. Decide what not to build. Judge each technical change by whether it made a user's job easier. AI-native teams name this judgement as the skill that is hardest to find.
